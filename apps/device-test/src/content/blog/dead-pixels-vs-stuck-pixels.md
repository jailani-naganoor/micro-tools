A tiny dot on your screen that never changes can be distracting, especially on a new monitor or laptop. Dead pixels and stuck pixels look similar at first, but they behave differently, and one is more likely to be fixable than the other.

## How a pixel works

Each pixel on an LCD or OLED screen is made of smaller subpixels, usually one red, one green and one blue. Mixing their brightness creates almost any colour: all on gives white, all off gives black. A pixel fault happens when one or more subpixels stops responding.

## What is a dead pixel?

A dead pixel stays dark all the time. It does not light up, whatever the screen is showing. It is easiest to spot on a white or bright background, where it appears as a small black dot.

Dead pixels usually come from a fault in the panel itself, such as a failed transistor that controls the pixel. Because of this, they are generally permanent.

## What is a stuck pixel?

A stuck pixel is lit when it shouldn't be, because one or more of its subpixels is stuck on. It usually shows as a single bright red, green or blue dot, or sometimes a mix such as yellow, cyan or magenta. It is easiest to see on a black or dark background.

A pixel that is permanently white (all subpixels stuck on) is sometimes called a hot pixel.

Stuck pixels are more likely than dead pixels to recover, either on their own or with the methods below, although there is no guarantee.

## Rule out dust and smudges first

Before testing, clean the screen. Turn the display off, then wipe it gently with a dry, soft microfibre cloth. A speck of dust or a dried drop of liquid can look very much like a faulty pixel. If the dot moves or disappears when you wipe it, it was not a pixel fault.

## How to test for dead and stuck pixels

The simplest test is to fill the whole screen with one solid colour at a time and look carefully for dots that don't match.

1. Set the screen to full brightness and view it straight on.
2. Show a full-screen **white** image. Look for dark dots (dead pixels).
3. Show full-screen **black**. Look for bright or coloured dots (stuck pixels).
4. Show full-screen **red**, **green** and **blue** in turn. A subpixel that is stuck off or stuck on will stand out against these colours.
5. Move your eyes slowly across the screen in sections, rather than trying to take in the whole screen at once.
6. Note the position of anything you find, and take a photo if you might need it for a support request.

## Can a stuck pixel be fixed?

Sometimes. Here are the approaches people commonly try, from the safest upwards.

### Wait and use the screen normally

Some stuck pixels clear up on their own after a few hours or days of normal use. If the display is new, this is worth trying before anything else.

### Rapidly changing colours

Some tools flash rapidly changing colours over the stuck area for a period of time, with the aim of getting the subpixel to respond again. Results vary and there is no guarantee it will work. If you try this:

- Only run it for a limited time, and check the result.
- **Do not look at rapidly flashing colours if you are sensitive to flashing light or have photosensitive epilepsy.** Cover the area or look away while it runs.

### Be very cautious with pressure methods

You may read advice about gently pressing or rubbing the stuck pixel with a soft cloth. Be careful. Pressing on an LCD or OLED panel can damage it and create more faulty pixels, a larger damaged area or permanent marks. On a laptop or a new monitor, physical pressure could also affect a warranty claim. If you are unsure, it is safer not to try pressure methods at all.

### Dead pixels

There is no reliable home fix for a truly dead pixel. If it bothers you, the realistic options are a warranty claim, a repair or a replacement.

## Check the manufacturer's pixel policy

Many display and laptop makers publish a pixel policy that explains how many faulty pixels, of which type and in which part of the screen, count as a defect under warranty. These policies differ between brands and sometimes between product lines, so check the policy for your exact model on the manufacturer's website.

If you have just bought the screen, check the shop's return policy too.

When you contact support, include:

- The model and serial number of the screen or laptop
- Where the fault is on the screen and what colour it shows
- Photos taken against solid colour backgrounds
- When you first noticed it

## Summary

A dead pixel is always dark and is usually permanent. A stuck pixel shows a bright colour and may recover. Clean the screen, test with solid colours, avoid pressing on the panel, and check your manufacturer's policy if the fault stays.
